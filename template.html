Why eight and not forty-one

The criterion was written before any results

A category is in scope only if it can close on its own, with evidence.

rule: end-to-end accuracy ≥ 65%
presence: balanced ≥ 70% and recall ≥ 70%
both: must beat the naive parse

Two categories fell when we measured the obvious

Warranty Duration and No-Solicit of Employees had balanced accuracy of 76% and 75.6% and passed the criterion. But answering "not present" every time scores 83% and 90% on those two: the product lost by 7 and 10 points. Out of scope.

Two engines, assigned by measurement

The two rows that look most trivial are where the naive parse collapses. The first line of a contract is almost never the title — it is Exhibit 10.4, EXECUTION COPY, a page number: 5% against 66%. And the first state named is almost never the governing law but a mailing address or a Delaware incorporation: 38% against 90%.

What it does not do

Not legal advice

It locates clauses and flags absences so a person can read them. It does not judge whether a clause is favourable.

A perfect sheet is rare

16% of sheets come out with no errors at all; 47% are 80% correct or better. Six or seven correct rows out of eight is the normal case.

Says whether, not always where

Rule-based rows jump to the exact paragraph. Presence rows show the highest-scoring passage with no guarantee it is the clause: on the held-out split, location is right between 12% and 46% of the time.
