How to read a row

Found, absent, review

Found and absent close on their own. Review means the clause is one where a wrong "absent" changes the economics of the deal, so the system proposes and a person confirms. It is never closed automatically, however sure the detector is.

Two kinds of confidence

A presence row carries a number calibrated for that row: how often the detector was right when it decided by that margin. A rule row has no per-row signal, so it carries the clause's measured accuracy and says so rather than dressing it up.

The sample is not a clean one

It is a real sponsorship agreement from the held-out split, and the sheet gets six of eight rows right on it: the attorney labels disagree on License Grant and Audit Rights. Sixteen per cent of sheets come out with no errors at all, so a perfect one would be the unrepresentative choice.

Two timings, and the gap between them

The function itself answers in single-digit milliseconds. What you waited for is that plus the trip to the region it runs in — from Mexico to us-east-1 the network is the overwhelming majority of it. Both numbers are on the page because only one of them is the experience.

Short contracts are out of distribution

Presence rows are scored over 1,600-character windows, and the corpus median is 32 KB. Paste two pages and the cue terms have almost nowhere to land: the rows will come back, and the presence ones will be unreliable in a way the confidence number does not capture.

Not legal advice

It locates clauses and flags absences so a person can read them. It does not judge whether a clause is favourable, and it is built for English-language contracts under US law.
